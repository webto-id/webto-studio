# Profil personal

## Who comes, and what they came to do

- Someone who heard the name (a client, an event organiser, a recruiter, a
  journalist) and wants to know who this is, whether they are good, and how
  to reach them. Often they arrive from an Instagram bio or a LinkedIn
  profile on a phone.
- A potential client comparing two or three freelancers: the work, the
  services, roughly what it costs, how to start.

## What decides it

- A clear sentence of who this person is and what they do, in their own
  voice.
- The work itself: three to eight pieces they are proud of, each with what
  the problem was and what they did, rather than thirty thumbnails.
- Credibility they can back: clients they may name, publications, talks,
  certifications.
- A face. A personal site without the person's photo feels like hiding.
- The way to work with them: services, the process, a price or "mulai dari"
  when they want to show it.

## The pages

- One page is often best: hero, work, about, services, contact.
- Separate Karya pages per project when each has a real story and images.
- A blog only when they write; a link to the newsletter or channel when that
  is where they write.

## The first screen

The name, what they do in one line, the portrait, and the one action that
matters for them (hire, book a talk, download the CV).

## The main action

Usually a message: WhatsApp or email, or a short form asking what the
project is and when. For a speaker: a booking request with the date and
the event. For a creator: the channel or the collaboration contact.

## Searches

- The name, which they should win outright: the name in the home title, the
  same spelling as on their social profiles, listed in
  `businessProfile.socialLinks`.
- profession + place for local work: `fotografer produk surabaya`, `desainer
  logo freelance bandung`, `psikolog anak jakarta selatan`.

## Rules of the trade

- Clients, employers and publications only with permission, and logos only
  when the owner may use them.
- Titles, degrees and licences exactly as held. Regulated professions
  (psychologist, doctor, lawyer, notary) follow their own playbook's rules
  too: `kesehatan`, `profesional`.
- Testimonials only with permission, verbatim.

## Photographs

Ask for: a recent portrait that looks like them today, work in context,
and them at work. A personal site lives on its portrait: if it is a poor
selfie, design with type and the work, and say in the handoff that a good
portrait would help most.

## Where the concept comes from

- Their medium: the manuscript, the contact sheet, the layer panel, the
  stage, the microphone, the whiteboard.
- Their voice: how they write their own captions and bios.
- Their path: where they started and where they work now, from the brief.
- Their signature habit or tool, when they name one.

## Clichés of this trade

- "Hi, I'm ..." with a waving hand emoji over a gradient.
- A circle-cropped portrait above three skill bars with percentages.
- A grid of tool logos (Figma, Photoshop, ...) as the "skills" section.
- Dark mode with neon accents as "creative".

## As a template

A fictional person with a profession, a city, a short honest bio, and
four pieces of work with a sentence each. The buyer replaces: the name, the
portrait, the work, the services. Design for a person with 3 projects and
with 30, with a long name, and with no portrait at all.
